After an accident: three steps

Make sure everyone is safe

Call 911 if anyone is hurt, a pedestrian or cyclist is involved, a driver will not cooperate or leaves the scene, impairment is suspected, or the scene is blocking traffic.

Report it, then call your insurer

If combined damage is over $5,000, report it at the Durham Collision Reporting Centre within 48 hours. Then tell your insurer — within seven days, or as soon as you reasonably can.

Choose your shop and send photos

The repair shop is your decision. Text photos of the damage to 905-730-6089 with your claim number and we will get the estimate moving.

Reporting a collision in Durham

Ontario raised the reporting threshold from $2,000 to $5,000 on 1 January 2025. Above that, the collision has to be reported in person at a Collision Reporting Centre within 48 hours.

What to bring to the reporting centre

When to call police instead

Do not use the reporting centre if anyone is seriously hurt, a pedestrian or cyclist was involved, a driver will not cooperate, there was a hit and run, impaired driving or no insurance is involved, a government vehicle or dangerous goods are involved, or the scene is a safety hazard. Collisions on Highways 401, 412 and 418 are handled by the OPP.

Who pays when it was not your fault

This is the part that confuses most people, so here it is in plain words.

If another insured driver caused the crash in Ontario, you usually claim the damage to your car from your own insurer. That is called Direct Compensation – Property Damage, or DCPD. It feels wrong the first time, but it is how the system is set up and it is normally faster.

If you were not at fault, you do not pay a deductible. If you were partly at fault, the deductible is shared according to the fault percentages. Since 2024, drivers have been able to opt out of DCPD coverage, so check your own policy or ask your broker.

Questions about insurance claims

Can I choose my own body shop if I claim insurance?

Yes. In Ontario you have the right to choose your repair shop, tow company and rental company. Your insurer may suggest a shop, but the choice is yours. Source: Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario.

Who pays if the accident was not my fault?

In Ontario you usually claim damage to your car from your own insurer, even when another driver caused it. This is called Direct Compensation – Property Damage. If you were not at fault you do not pay a deductible. Since 2024 drivers can opt out of this coverage, so check your own policy.

When do I have to report a collision?

Report it at a Collision Reporting Centre within 48 hours when combined damage is over $5,000. Under that amount no police report is needed, but you should still tell your insurer. Call 911 instead if anyone is hurt, a driver leaves the scene or impairment is suspected.

What do I need to bring for an insurance repair?

Your claim number, your adjuster’s name and contact details, your insurance card and the car keys. If you reported at the Collision Reporting Centre, bring that report number too.

How soon do I have to tell my insurer?

Tell your insurer within seven days or as soon as you reasonably can. Source: Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario.
