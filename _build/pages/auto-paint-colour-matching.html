Why the booth matters

Paint sprayed in open air picks up dust and dries unevenly. A proper booth controls the air, the dust and the temperature, which is what gives a repair the same depth of shine as the factory finish.

How colour matching works

Find the code

Every vehicle carries a paint code on a sticker in the door jamb or under the hood. That is where the match starts.

Match to the car

Paint ages. The code is the starting point, then the mix is adjusted to the car in front of us, not the car it was when new.

Prep and mask

The surface is flattened and primed, and everything that is not being painted is masked off properly.

Spray and blend

Colour is laid in the booth and blended into the panels beside the repair, so there is no hard edge for your eye to catch.

How pricing works

Paint pricing comes down to how many panels are involved, how much prep the surface needs, and how far the colour has to be blended to look right. A scuff on one bumper corner is a different job from a repair that crosses a door and a fender.

Questions about paint

Will the new paint match my car?

Matching your colour and finish is the goal on every job. We work from your vehicle’s paint code and blend the new paint into the panels beside the repair so there is no hard edge. A trade customer who sends us vehicles says the paint always comes out a perfect match and the same shade.

Can you paint just one panel?

Yes. Painting one panel and blending into the ones next to it is routine. Blending matters because the same paint code can look slightly different on a car that has spent years in the sun.

Why does my car’s paint look different from the paint code?

Paint fades and changes with age, sunlight and washing. That is why a repair is blended into the surrounding panels rather than sprayed to a hard edge, so your eye reads it as one colour.

How long before I can wash the car?

Fresh paint needs time to fully harden. We will tell you when you pick the car up how long to wait and what to avoid in the meantime.
